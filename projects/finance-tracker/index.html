A private, personal finance management PWA built around real-life financial workflows.

Project scope: I built this application for my own personal use. It is an invitation-only, small-scale project — not a commercial finance service, enterprise platform, or production banking product.

1. The problem

I wanted a clearer picture of my finances than a basic monthly expense tracker could provide. My financial life includes day-to-day spending, savings, investments, education-loan repayments, family support, multiple accounts and financial reporting. Treating every movement of money as income or expense can make reports misleading — for example, moving money between my own accounts is not spending, and money allocated toward an investment should not be treated like an ordinary purchase.

I also wanted budgeting rules that matched my circumstances. A standard 50/30/20 framework is a useful benchmark, but someone managing an education loan may prefer a loan-focused plan. I needed the ability to choose a plan and record financial activity in a way that preserves the meaning of each transaction.

2. The solution

I designed and built a private finance tracker that brings transaction entry, budgeting, accounts, analytics and statements into one responsive web application that can also be installed as a Progressive Web App (PWA).

The application is designed around manual, user-controlled financial records. I enter and review transactions instead of connecting bank accounts or importing sensitive financial activity automatically. Authentication and invitation-only registration keep the application private.

The objective was not to reproduce every feature of a commercial finance platform. It was to build a practical tool tailored to the financial workflows I actually need.

3. Key features

  • Dashboard and overviewKey figures and charts for income, expenses, savings, investments and cash flow.
  • Transactions and accountsRecord activity across accounts while distinguishing income, expenses and transfers.
  • Flexible budgetingStandard 50/30/20, a loan-focused 40/20/20/20 plan, or custom percentages that total 100%.
  • Education-loan trackingRecord repayments and monitor the loan within the broader financial picture.
  • Investments and provident fundInvestment and PF amounts stay distinguishable from ordinary spending.
  • Family-support trackingSupport received from family is recorded separately from support given.
  • Analytics and statementsTrends plus income/expense, cash-flow and related reports.
  • PDF and Excel exportsExport financial information for review and recordkeeping.
  • Privacy and access controlsAuthentication, invitation-based registration and a PIN lock.
  • Responsive and installableWorks on desktop or mobile as a web app / PWA.

Savings Goals and Expense Projects (for example saving for a laptop or analysing a trip) are future enhancements, not current features.

4. Key design decision: classify money movement correctly

One of the central challenges was representing financial activity without double-counting it.

ActivityHow the app treats itWhy it matters
Salary receivedIncomeRecords money earned
Transfer between my own accountsTransferDoes not inflate income or expenses
Food or travel purchaseExpenseContributes to spending analysis
Investment contributionInvestment / savings trackingKept distinct from normal consumption
Education-loan paymentLoan repaymentMakes debt payments visible separately
Family support received or givenSeparate family-support flowShows family-related money movement clearly

I keep the budget framework separate from transaction classification: the framework defines the plan, while transactions record what actually happened.

The project uses MongoDB Atlas's free cluster (documented 512 MB storage limit). This was an intentional low-cost choice for a personal-scale application, not a claim that the deployment is sized for high traffic, large datasets or enterprise use.

6. My role and approach

I drove the project from personal-finance requirements through product decisions to a working deployment:

  • identifying the financial questions the app needed to answer;
  • defining how different transaction types should be classified;
  • deciding how budgeting, loan tracking, accounts and reports work together;
  • shaping the responsive interface and private-access experience;
  • integrating the application with its database and deployment environment;
  • testing the main workflows locally and checking the deployed application.

I used AI assistance during development for coding guidance, debugging and iteration. I remained responsible for defining the requirements, evaluating the proposed changes and checking that the resulting workflows matched the intended financial logic.

7. Outcome

The outcome is a deployed, working personal tool that brings several parts of my financial recordkeeping into one place. It shows how I translated a real-world finance problem into software rules, data structures, user workflows and reporting screens.

It also gave me practice in full-stack product development: moving from requirements to implementation, resolving workflow and interface issues, testing changes, and deploying a private application. I do not claim quantified savings, investment performance or business impact, because I have not established independently measured results for those outcomes.

8. Why build it instead of using an existing app?

Established products such as YNAB, Wallet by BudgetBakers and Monarch Money offer broader capabilities, and this project is not meant to compete with them feature-for-feature. I chose to build my own tracker for fit and control: my exact budget presets, separate education-loan and family-support views, manual transaction review, and reporting shaped around my own financial habits — and as a way to learn by solving a concrete finance problem.

9. Limitations and next steps

This is a personal-scale project. It does not connect to banks, move money, provide financial advice, or claim bank-grade or enterprise-grade security. The MongoDB free tier has limited capacity and no paid-tier backup features, so important records should be backed up separately.

Roadmap ideas: a more flexible dashboard period selector, savings goals for major purchases, expense projects for trips or events, and further automated tests. These are ideas, not current functionality.

Takeaway: Personal Finance Tracker is a practical example of finance-led problem solving through software. I identified a real gap in my own workflow, modelled the financial distinctions that mattered, and built and deployed a tool around them.
